\section{Maximum of the Edge Error}\label{sec:MaxErrorDerivation}
We derive \cref{eq:MaxError} of the paper for $r > 1$.
The derivative of the relative error is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}\delta}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{(r - 1)\,\bigl((r - 1)\,t^2 - 2r\,t + r\bigr)}{\bigl(r - (r - 1)\,t\bigr)^2}.
\end{equation}
Its numerator vanishes at $t = (r \pm \sqrt{r})/(r - 1)$.
Only the smaller root, $t^\ast = (r - \sqrt{r})/(r - 1) = \sqrt{r}/(\sqrt{r} + 1)$, lies in $[0, 1]$.
With $1 - t^\ast = 1/(\sqrt{r} + 1)$ and $r - (r - 1)\,t^\ast = \sqrt{r}$, the maximum is
\begin{equation}
    \delta(t^\ast) = \frac{(r - 1)\,\sqrt{r}}{(\sqrt{r} + 1)^2\,\sqrt{r}} = \frac{\sqrt{r} - 1}{\sqrt{r} + 1}.
\end{equation}

\section{Error Inside a Triangle}\label{sec:InteriorError}
In \cref{sec:EdgeError} of the paper, we claim that the error inside a triangle never exceeds the largest error along its edges.
We test the claim with \num{3000} random triangles.
Their depths $w_k$ are uniform in $[1, 10]$, their texture coordinates $\vect{u}_k$ uniform in $[0, 1]^2$.
For each triangle, we evaluate the error $\Vert \vect{u}_\mathrm{aff} - \vect{u} \Vert$ with the weights of \cref{eq:Weights}~\cite{Heckbert91IPT} at the \num{19701} interior points of a barycentric grid with the spacing $1/200$.
We divide the largest error by the largest error along the edges, $\ell\,\delta_\mathrm{max}$ for each edge.
The largest ratio over all triangles is 0.999869, close to but below one.
The floor confirms the claim, too: its measured errors match the edge errors in \cref{tab:Subdivision}.
